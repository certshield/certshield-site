About the Official AWS Certified Data Engineer - Associate Exam

Sourced directly from the official AWS Certified Data Engineer - Associate Exam Guide, published by Amazon Web Services (AWS) — Version 1.1, effective December 12, 2025.

Questions
65 total questions (50 count toward the score; 15 are unscored pretest items used by AWS to evaluate future exam content and are not identified to the candidate)
Time Limit
130 minutes (2 hours 10 minutes)
Passing Score
720 on a scaled 100–1,000 range (compensatory scoring across the whole exam; no minimum is required in any individual domain)
Registration Fee
$150 USD (holders of an active AWS certification may be eligible for a 50% discount voucher)
Delivery
Pearson VUE — available both at an authorized testing center and via online proctoring
Validity
3-year certification cycle; recertify by passing the current version of this exam, or any AWS certification exam at the same or a higher tier, before expiration

Official exam domains this diagnostic mirrors:

  • Data Ingestion and Transformation 34%

Who this certification is for:

AWS recommends the equivalent of 2–3 years of experience in data engineering, including at least 1–2 years of hands-on experience with AWS services; the target candidate should understand the effects of volume, variety, and velocity on data ingestion, transformation, modeling, security, governance, privacy, schema design, and optimal data store design.

Performing ML training and inference, demonstrating knowledge of programming language-specific syntax, and drawing business conclusions from data are explicitly listed as out of scope. Version 1.1 (effective December 12, 2025) consolidated the guide's separate knowledge/skill lists into a single skills list per task and added coverage of LLM integration for data processing, open table formats (Apache Iceberg), vector index types (HNSW, IVF), vectorization concepts, and Amazon SageMaker Catalog/Unified Studio governance -- domain weights and total question counts were unchanged by this revision.
