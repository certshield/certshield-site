Why a range, and not one number?

A practice test is a small sample of everything an exam could ask. Which 30 questions you happen to get changes your score even when your knowledge has not changed. The range shows how much of your score is knowledge and how much is the luck of the draw.

The more questions you answer, the narrower the range becomes. The table under your result shows it: the same percentage on a longer test.

How to read your range

  • Trust the low end first. If even the low end clears the score you are aiming for, that is good news. If only the high end does, it is not settled yet.
  • A wide range means too little evidence. Answer more questions, or add another practice test, before deciding anything.
  • With results by topic, a topic you have not answered at all widens the range instead of being ignored.

What this does not tell you

  • It is not a chance of passing. Many exams report a scaled score, or only pass or fail, instead of a percentage of questions, so a practice-test percentage cannot be turned into a pass mark.
  • It reflects sampling luck only. It cannot see whether a practice test is easier or harder than the real exam, or whether its questions are well written.
  • It assumes your answers were your own, in one sitting, without looking things up.
